**Short version:** two big ones today. **The whole road-sign library now reads in
Afrikaans** — every sign, every part of every lesson, where before an Afrikaans
learner got English text on all of them. And **the AI coach now runs on the free
test**, so every visitor meets it instead of only the people who have paid — that
was the one thing making us more than a quiz app, and until today it was hidden
behind the paywall. We also fixed three problems only Afrikaans users had, and
cleaned up some clumsy English we had written ourselves. The AI costs about **5c per
learner**, with a **R20 a day** ceiling. Two things we need from you at the bottom.

## What we did today

| Update | Details |
| -- | -- |
| **The whole sign library now reads in Afrikaans** | Every one of the **373 signs** that has a written lesson now has that lesson in Afrikaans — all of it, not just the headings: what the sign means, what you must actually do at the wheel, the mistake learners make, the test hint and the memory trick. That is **1 914 pieces of text**. Until today an Afrikaans learner opened a sign and got English on every one of them. ⚠️ **This is our translation, not yours — it has not been checked by an Afrikaans speaker.** It reads well to us and we kept the wording consistent on purpose (one Afrikaans word per English word, right through), but you are the one who can say whether a South African would actually say it that way. |
| **We fixed clumsy English we had written ourselves** | While translating we found 23 signs where the **English** read badly — things like "Only park here if your vehicle is buses". That came from the way we generated those signs originally. All rewritten. Worth saying plainly: for a while, those signs will read better in Afrikaans than in English. |
| **The AI coach now runs on the free test** | Finish the free five-question test and you get a short, personal read from the coach: where you stand, the one or two things costing you marks, and a 10-minute plan pointing at the right lessons. Until today that page showed a score and an *example* of what the AI would have said — the real thing only existed after someone paid R179. This is the part that makes us more than another quiz app, and it was hidden behind the paywall, at exactly the moment people are deciding whether to buy. |
| **What it costs, and the ceiling on it** | About **5c per learner**. We set a **R20 a day** ceiling, which is roughly **400 free assessments a day** — far more free traffic than the site sees today. If a day ever goes past that, nobody sees an error: later visitors still get a useful summary built from their own scores. So a busy day, or somebody trying to abuse it, cannot run up a bill. |
| **The AI now writes in Afrikaans for people who paid** | The coach's report after a mock exam was coming back in English even on the Afrikaans site — Afrikaans headings wrapped around English advice. It now writes in Afrikaans throughout. |
| **Switching language no longer wipes your report** | Reading your report in Afrikaans used to delete the English one, and vice versa — going back cost another wait while it was written again. Both versions are now kept side by side. |
| **A brief outage no longer costs you the AI report** | If the AI was unavailable for even half a minute at the moment a learner asked for their report, they got the simpler summary — and were stuck with it forever, even after the AI came back. Now it simply tries again, and there is a "Try again" button when it would help. |
| **We tightened what the coach is allowed to say** | Six specific habits are now blocked rather than merely discouraged: it can no longer mention our own working notes to a learner, call a section your "best" when you failed it, fill a compliment slot with something that says nothing ("you're not starting from zero"), repeat the same study step twice, send you to revise a section you already passed, or run on at length. Where a rule was only asked for, the model could quietly ignore it — now the software checks the report before a learner ever sees it. |
| **Tested with real assessments, in both languages** | Eight complete assessments — four on the free test and four on real mock papers, English and Afrikaans. All eight passed the new checks, so the stricter rules did not push anyone onto the simpler summary. On a 94% paper the compliments now say three different things instead of repeating one; on a 36% paper it gives no false praise at all. |
| **A correction to our own records** | Our notes said nobody had ever sat a mock exam on the live site. That was wrong — **you did, on 5 August, and passed with 89%.** The note was a day out of date when it was written and had been repeated in three other documents. All corrected. |
