#### VC-013 — SOUND
citation: NRTR reg 198(6)(a)(i) and reg 198(6)(b)
quote: "The driver of a motor vehicle fitted with a separate switch to operate all the direction indicators simultaneously, shall put into operation simultaneously all the direction indicators fitted to such vehicle, when the vehicle is— (i) stationary in a hazardous position; or (ii) in motion in an emergency situation. (b) The driver of a motor vehicle shall not put into operation simultaneously all the direction indicators fitted to such motor vehicle in a circumstance other than those referred to in paragraph (a)."
note: key language mirrors the reg almost verbatim. Distractor (1) is affirmatively prohibited by 198(6)(b); distractor (2) is prohibited both by 198(6)(b) and by the stopping regs. `vehicle_codes: B` is correctly scoped — reg 198(5)(b)(iii) exempts motor cycles from having a simultaneous-operation switch at all.

#### VC-014 — SOUND
citation: NRTR reg 157(3); distractor (2) additionally killed by reg 157(1)(b)
quote: "A person operating a motor vehicle on a public road shall extinguish the main-beam of the light emitted by the head lamp of such vehicle if such main-beam could cause a dangerous glare to oncoming traffic."
quote: "the head lamps, rear lamps and number plate lamps are kept lighted during the period between sunset and sunrise and at any other time when, due to insufficient light or unfavourable weather conditions, persons and vehicles upon the public road are not clearly discernible at a distance of 150 metres"
note: the explanation's "return to main beam once it has passed" is technique, not law, but is not asserted as a legal requirement. Motor cycles (code A) are in scope correctly — reg 159(1) requires a motor cycle head lamp "capable of emitting a main-beam and a dipped-beam". No number is stated in the explanation, so nothing to fabricate.

#### VC-015 — SOUND
citation: NRTR reg 326(1)(a), with the "before" timing fixed by regs 324 and 325
quote: "The driver of a vehicle which is fitted with direction indicators in terms of the provisions of regulations 193 to 199 shall signal his or her intention to turn or move to the left or right by operating the direction indicator on the left or right side, as the case may be, of the vehicle."
quote: "the driver of a vehicle on a public road who intends to turn to the right or move to the right shall, before reaching the point at which he or she intends to turn or move to the right, extend his or her right arm and hand sideward from the shoulder..."
note: the explanation's claim "you must signal regardless of whether another vehicle is near" is genuinely supported — reg 326(1)(a) is unconditional and contains no traffic-present qualifier, so distractor (1) is legally wrong, not merely poor practice. Distractor (0) fails the "before reaching the point" timing in regs 324/325. "In good time" is K53 phrasing, not a statutory interval, and no number is claimed.

#### VC-018 — SOUND
citation: none for the technique. NRTR reg 204(1)(b) is an *equipment* requirement only and does not govern reversing procedure
quote: "which is not fitted with a rearview mirror or mirrors enabling the driver of such vehicle, when he or she is in the driving position, to see in clear weather a clear reflection of traffic to the rear"
note: there is no reversing-procedure provision anywhere in the rules-of-the-road Part (grepped "revers*" across the whole instrument — every hit is a reversing *lamp*, a reversing *mechanism*, or a seatbelt exemption). The explanation correctly does not claim one. Loose wording only: "the demister does not provide vision" — a rear-screen demister does materially aid rearward vision through a misted window. Not enough to make it a defensible answer to "which controls help you see", so not AMBIGUOUS, but "do not show you what is behind you" would be the more exact phrasing.
