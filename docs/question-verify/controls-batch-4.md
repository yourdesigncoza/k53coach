# c4 — adversarial review (VC-020 … VC-027)

#### VC-020 — SOUND
citation: NRTR reg 201(1)(a); NRTR reg 310A
quote: "no person shall operate on a public road— (a) a self-propelled motor vehicle, unless it is equipped with an efficient warning device which is in good working order and, when used, capable of emitting a sound which, under normal conditions, is clearly audible by a person of normal hearing from a distance of at least 90 metres"
quote: "310A. No person shall on a public road use the sounding device or hooter of a vehicle except when such use is necessary in order to comply with the provisions of these regulations or on the grounds of safety."
issue: none. Both distractors are genuinely wrong (indicator signals intention, wipers clear glass). The stem's "warn ... of danger" maps exactly onto reg 310A's "on the grounds of safety". No number asserted.

#### VC-021 — SOUND
citation: NRTR reg 1 (definition of "stop lamp"); NRTR reg 168(1)
quote: "\"stop lamp\" means a device fitted to a vehicle for the purpose of signalling, by means of a light, the intention of the driver of such vehicle to stop or reduce the speed of such vehicle"
quote: "168. (1) No person shall operate a motor vehicle, other than a trailer drawn by a tractor or a tractor, on a public road unless it is fitted with at least one stop lamp on each side at the rear of the motor vehicle, and in the case of a motor cycle one stop lamp at the rear"
issue: none. Headlamps (regs 159–161) and parking lamps (reg 164) are separate devices under separate regs; neither is brake-actuated. Explanation asserts no number.

#### VC-022 — SOUND
citation: none — technique, not law
issue: none material. "Disconnects the engine" is loose (it disconnects the engine *from the driven wheels*), but it is not a misstatement in ordinary usage. Both distractors are plainly false.

#### VC-023 — SOUND
citation: none — technique, not law
issue: none. (NRTR reg 1 defines "parking brake" as "a brake, normally a hand brake, used in the ordinary course of events to keep a vehicle stationary", and reg 155(1)(c) sets its holding performance on a gradient — but neither prescribes hill-start technique, so neither is a citation for this item.) Accelerator alone does not hold a stationary vehicle; demister is irrelevant. No number asserted.

#### VC-024 — SOUND
citation: none — technique, not law
issue: none. No demister/head-restraint provision exists in the NRTR (reg 203 covers windscreen wipers only, reg 202 glass). The stem correctly scopes the mist to the *inside* of the glass, which excludes a rear wiper as an alternative answer.

#### VC-025 — EXPLANATION_DEFECT
citation: NRTR reg 213(4)
quote: "(4) No adult shall occupy a seat in a motor vehicle operated on a public road which is fitted with a seatbelt unless such person wears such seatbelt: Provided that the provisions of this regulation do not apply while reversing or moving in or out of a parking bay or area."
issue: The marked answer is the best of the three, but the explanation's "must be fastened before the vehicle moves off and worn **at all times**" is an over-broad legal claim contradicted by the express proviso in reg 213(4) (reversing / moving in or out of a parking bay) and by reg 213(5) (an adult may lawfully occupy an unbelted seat once all belted seats on that row are taken). "The driver and passengers" is likewise wider than the regulation, which binds an adult occupying a seat *that is fitted with* a belt. Fix: drop "at all times", or qualify — e.g. "worn whenever the vehicle is being driven on a public road (the regulation excepts reversing and manoeuvring in a parking area)".

#### VC-026 — SOUND
citation: none — technique/vehicle safety, not law
issue: none. Grep of the NRTR returns no head-restraint or headrest provision at all, so no citation exists and none is claimed. The stem's "safety purpose" scoping is what makes distractor (0) unambiguously wrong — a head restraint does also serve comfort, and without that scoping the item would be AMBIGUOUS. Keep the wording as-is.

#### VC-027 — EXPLANATION_DEFECT
citation: none for the hand/foot allocation — convention, not law. NRTR reg 150 requires only two independent systems, not which limb operates them.
quote: "150. No person shall operate on a public road a motor cycle, motor tricycle or motor quadrucycle which is not equipped with two independent braking systems, one of which shall act on the front wheel or wheels and the other which shall act on the rear wheel or wheels"
issue: The marked answer holds universally (right lever = front brake on manual motorcycles *and* on scooters), and both distractors are wrong in both configurations. But the explanation's second clause — "the rear brake is the right-foot pedal and the clutch is the left-hand lever" — is false for twist-and-go scooters/automatics, which are inside `vehicle_codes: A`: there is no clutch lever and no foot pedal, and the left lever is the rear brake. A learner on a scooter would hunt for controls that do not exist. Fix: scope it, e.g. "on a conventional manual motorcycle the rear brake is the right-foot pedal and the clutch the left-hand lever; on a scooter the left lever is the rear brake".
