## Things that will bite whoever picks this up

**`resources/` is now load-bearing and untracked.** `RR28` cites Vol 2 Ch 7, the markings cite Vol 1 Ch 7,
three signs rest on Vol 1 C2/C5 — and a fresh clone can verify none of it. All 22 PDFs came from
`https://www.transport.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/V<vol>C<ch>.pdf`. Volume 1 = the sign
catalogue, Volume 2 = applications, **Volume 3 = Traffic Signal Design and is not the sign
catalogue** — that assumption cost a detour today.

**A withdrawn question is not a withdrawn claim.** `RS-041` was pulled on 30 July for describing the
blue pedestrian crossing as South African. The *sign* stayed served for another day, and was still
live when John spotted it in the UI. When an item is pulled for a content reason, check the library.

**Inherited facts are the ones that bite.** Three separate claims failed today because they were
carried forward without checking: `W318` as "the uncontrolled crossing" (it is an advance warning
sign, used at every protection class), reg 297(2)'s three routes (substituted in 2001, one route),
and the boom as a form of crossing control (it is a warning device). Each came from a summary that
read as settled.

**Absence from an extraction is not absence from the source.** This nearly caused real damage twice:
a naive code match flagged 156 served signs as ungrounded, then 99, then 66 — and of the final 66
only two were genuinely foreign. Separately, 16 markings look absent from the chart text because
sheet 2 draws them as vector without exposing labels. Both times the mass action would have been
wrong. Withdraw on positive evidence, never on silence.

**The `ai:claude-code+brave` approver marks a weaker gate.** 100 served signs carry it — the fallback
that ran a web search when the chart lacked the sign, citing third-party sites. That path admitted
`R360`. Any future ingest should refuse to approve where `in_official_chart` is false.
