## South Africa's New Learner's Licence Testing System

South Africa's Western Cape province introduced a new computerised learner's licence testing system in May 2025, but it has resulted in extremely low pass rates with only 17% of candidates passing.

**Key Details:**
- **Pass rate:** 17% overall (2 out of 10 candidates pass) - this is a 46-point drop from the previous 63% average
- **New system features:** Multiple-choice questions from randomized pools instead of memorization-based questions

**Why It Matters:**
- The new system aims to reduce cheating by eliminating physical question booklets and manual marking
- Computerised testing provides instant results and eliminates marking errors
- The system requires applicants to demonstrate better understanding of road rules and signs

**Key Concerns Raised:**
| Issue | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Language barriers** | Questions not assessed for language proficiency despite being available in 11 official South African languages |
| **Lack of preparation** | Candidates struggle with understanding question wording and translated content |
| **Transition challenges** | Department of Mobility attributes lower pass rates partly to the shift from manual to computerised testing |


**System Impact:**
- Eliminates widespread cheating that occurred in the previous manual system
- Reduces time needed for testing (instant results)
- Creates more standardized testing across all centers
- Requires candidates to demonstrate comprehensive understanding rather than rote memorization
